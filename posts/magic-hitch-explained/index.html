Magic Hitch, explained: how a trailer hitches itself

Hitching is the most-hated two minutes of any trip. The Flow tries to erase them entirely with cameras and AI. Here’s what’s actually happening in that demo.

The clip that made the rounds looks almost fake: a driver pulls the tow vehicle up roughly in front of the trailer, walks away, taps a phone — and the Flow rolls itself the last few feet, lines up, and clicks onto the ball. No spotter, no ten-point reversing dance.

What’s the trailer actually “seeing”?

Magic Hitch is a vision + AI system. Cameras on the trailer locate the tow vehicle’s hitch point, estimate the distance and angle, and then the dual-motor drivetrain nudges the Flow into precise alignment. Because the trailer has its own motors (part of the Magic Pack), it doesn’t need you to inch the truck backward — it closes the gap itself.

The insight is that a self-propelled trailer can solve the alignment problem from its own end, instead of relying on the driver to aim a two-ton vehicle in a mirror.

Why it matters more than it looks

Hitching is where a lot of driveway fender-benders and jack-knifed trailers happen. Automating it isn’t just a flex — it removes the single most stressful, error-prone moment of a trip, and it makes towing approachable for people who’d never attempt to back a trailer at all.
